# Role cards

Assign four to seven roles. One person may hold two roles in a small group, but
the incident lead should remain separate.

## Incident lead

Maintain a shared picture of impact, decisions, owners, and next review time.
Choose whether to continue, degrade, pause, or roll back the feature.

## Product owner

Represent intended behaviour, affected workflows, and acceptable product
trade-offs. Clarify who should and should not use the feature.

## Engineering and operations

Investigate the system path, propose containment and recovery, and explain what
telemetry or rollback capability actually exists.

## Security and privacy

Assess adversarial behaviour, data exposure, access boundaries, evidence
preservation, and reporting obligations. Avoid assuming every AI failure is a
security incident.

## Design and user experience

Represent what users can understand and control. Examine degraded states,
warnings, correction, and recovery in the interface.

## Customer support

Bring user reports into the incident, identify communication needs, and
translate response decisions into useful guidance.

## Communications or legal

Consider notification, factual accuracy, regulatory or contractual duties, and
the risk of saying too much or too little before facts are known.

## Observer

Record decisions and evidence gaps. Do not solve the scenario. Watch for
unclear ownership, missing users, and claims that cannot be verified.
